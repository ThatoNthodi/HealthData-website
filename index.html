Why multi-site health data goes wrong

Mixed units

One site reports glucose in mg/dL and another in mmol/L. Combined, one site looks about 18 times sicker.

Different formats and codes

Day-first and month-first dates, "M", "Male" and "1" for the same thing, and trailing spaces nobody sees.

Gaps and impossible values

Duplicate records, blanks, and entries like a blood pressure of 900 or an age of minus 4.

How it works

  1. Week 1: Agree and profileWe sign a scope of work and a data processing agreement. We fingerprint your original files so we can prove they were not changed, then profile each site.
  2. Week 2: Draft and test the rulesWe write the standard rules and test them on a sample, checking that nothing is silently deleted.

Typical timeline for up to three sites. Larger projects are quoted separately.

How we protect your data

Originals stay untouched

Work happens on copies. Checksums show the original files were never altered.

Written agreement first

No personal information moves until a data processing agreement is signed and you have confirmed your lawful basis for sharing it.

Only the people who need it

What this is, and what it is not

This is a data preparation service. We do not build or validate predictive models, give clinical advice, or certify that your data or organisation meets any law. Your clinicians and privacy officer keep the final sign-off. The score reflects specific automated checks. It does not guarantee that data is clinically valid, unbiased or safe for deployment.

Questions

Do you need patient names and ID numbers?

No. We ask for coded or de-identified data wherever possible. If direct identifiers are needed, that is agreed in writing first.

Can you build the prediction model too?

Not in this assessment. It prepares and checks the data. Modelling can be discussed separately once the data is ready.

What does a high score mean?

That the data passed our specific checks for quality, leakage and privacy. It does not mean the data is clinically valid, unbiased or safe to deploy. The weights and thresholds behind the score are written in a plain file your clinicians and governance team can review and challenge.

What does it cost?

A fixed fee, agreed after a short call so we know how many sites and how much data are involved.

Who reviews the rules?

Your own clinicians. We draft the rules in a plain file so they can read, question and approve each one.
